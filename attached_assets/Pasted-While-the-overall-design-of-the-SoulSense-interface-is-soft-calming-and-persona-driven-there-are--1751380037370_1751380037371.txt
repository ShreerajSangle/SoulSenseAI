While the overall design of the SoulSense interface is soft, calming, and persona-driven, there are several areas that can be improved to enhance clarity, usability, and emotional immersion. Firstly, the current placement and styling of timestamps (e.g., 15:24:25 and 15:24:31) appear redundant and visually distracting; a cleaner approach would be to display timestamps only on hover or group them per message block for better readability. Additionally, the visibility of inactive personas like Alex, Maya, and Marcus in the blurred background may confuse users about who is actively responding. This can be addressed by dimming or blurring the inactive cards further and highlighting the active persona’s card with a glowing border or visual focus. Although the response is clearly labeled with "DS" for Dr. Sarah, the interface lacks strong contextual reinforcement of the persona in focus. Adding a visible banner such as “You’re chatting with Dr. Sarah” or displaying her therapeutic style (e.g., CBT-focused, calm) on hover would create stronger contextual anchoring.

The quick-response buttons also feel generic and not tailored to the mental health domain. Phrases like “Can you explain differently?” may work in educational tools but feel misaligned in emotional conversations. Instead, these buttons should be dynamically personalized based on the active persona—such as “Can you reframe that for me?” for Sarah, “Say that in a softer way” for Maya, “Give me a plan” for Marcus, or “Say that with a joke 😄” for Alex. Another UI issue lies in the visual imbalance between user and assistant messages. The user’s “Hey” bubble appears tightly packed and overly right-aligned compared to Dr. Sarah’s more spacious message. Consistent padding and vertical alignment should be applied for visual harmony. Additionally, while the "DS" icon identifies the persona, the chat lacks expressive avatars or visual cues that humanize the interaction—adding a persona thumbnail or even optional animated illustrations could make the experience more engaging and emotionally resonant.

The interface also lacks navigational tools for viewing or editing past interactions. Users would benefit from features like an expandable chat thread, a “view session summary” option, or the ability to revise previous inputs. Lastly, while the system likely detects emotion in user input, there’s no visual feedback provided to the user. Introducing a soft emotion tag or emoji beside their message—e.g., "😔 sadness detected"—could improve transparency and allow users to correct misread emotions if needed. Together, these changes would make SoulSense more emotionally intelligent, visually cohesive, and contextually responsive—key for a therapeutic AI experience.
